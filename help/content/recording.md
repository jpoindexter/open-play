## Capture the master mix

1. Open **REC / Recording**.
2. Select **Master mix** as the source and give the capture a name.
3. Choose **Start recording** before the section you want to keep.
4. Play and mix local tracks.
5. Choose **Stop recording**, wait for the capture to finish and select **Download capture**.
6. Confirm the downloaded file exists and plays before quitting or upgrading.

The actual recorder captures the browser engine’s master stream. The browser chooses a supported codec, typically WebM/Opus or a supported fallback. The download extension follows the capture container. Do not rename an extension to claim a format conversion.

## What is not captured

**Mic — simulation** and **Aux — simulation** are labelled fixtures. Choosing one does not capture a real external input; actual capture remains the master mix. Native AIFF/WAV, pre-master recording and automatic splitting are not established public features.

The **disk-full recovery** and **split** demonstration controls simulate status messages. They do not prove a disk failure was induced or a split/recovery recording was produced.

## Keep a recording safe

An in-memory capture is not a durable backup. Download it before quitting, reloading, changing builds or attempting recovery. Metadata backup does not include recordings.

If recording fails, preserve any existing downloadable capture and the original audio. Note the version, output setup, approximate time and visible error. Avoid repeated destructive restarts.

## Use a capture when reporting a pop

Describe whether the defect was audible through speakers/headphones, present in the downloaded master capture, visible only in waveforms, or uncertain. Those observations help distinguish output, transport and display problems.

Do not upload copyrighted songs or full mixes by default. Use a concise description and privacy-reviewed screenshot first; agree a suitable diagnostic excerpt if needed. [Reporting guide](/known-issues/#report-a-problem).
